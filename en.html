Market guides

Practical guides to digital buying and selling

Start selling

What is a digital product — and how do you start selling your first one?

A digital product is an asset made once and sold many times, with no stock and no shipping: an ebook, a template, a recorded course or a ready working file. Its starting point is not the question "what do I sell?" but "what problem do I solve for others every week without noticing?"

Turn a repeated skill into an asset: the template you build your proposals on, the content sheet you run your accounts with, the checklist you review your work against before delivery. Gather the files in a clear order, write an honest description of what the product contains and what it does not, and attach a real preview from the product itself — not marketing promises.

Finish with a written licence from day one: personal or commercial use? One project or many? Licence clarity protects you from disputes and raises buyer trust — and on Souq Raqami it is a basic listing condition, not an optional extra.

  1. Define one problem the product solves for a clear audience
  2. Gather the files, order them, and name them understandably
  3. Write an honest description and preview, and a clear licence
  4. Publish, collect the first reviews, and update the product based on them
Pricing

How do you price a digital product without wronging yourself or your buyers?

Do not price by your hours; the buyer is not buying your time, but the result they reach faster because of your product. The right starting point is one question: what is the value of the time or the mistake this product saves its user?

Study your category's range in the market and place your product consciously by depth, scope and support, then count your silent costs: the sales channel's fees, the licences of assets you used to build the product, and support time after the sale. A price that does not cover these costs looks cheap today and collapses tomorrow.

Build at least two tiers: a basic edition for personal use, and a wider one with a commercial licence or extra files. And never enter the race to the bottom: whoever hunts for the cheapest pirated copy was never your customer, and random price cuts attract buyers the product does not fit — and unfair reviews with them.

  1. Price from the value of the result, not from work hours
  2. Study the category range and place yourself consciously
  3. Cover fees, asset licences and support time
  4. Offer two tiers: basic, and wider with a bigger licence
Buyer safety

Buying safely from digital marketplaces: five rules that never break

Most buyer losses in digital marketplaces do not come from a bad product, but from a purchase that left the rules: payment outside the platform, a licence never read, or an "original" copy at an impossible price. The five rules below close those three doors.

The most important rule: do everything inside the platform — payment, download and messaging. A seller who proposes completing the deal outside it is effectively asking you to give up your protection, your invoice and your written licence. And judge the seller by verified buyers' reviews and a real preview, not by a displayed sales count alone.

After buying: download immediately, keep an organised backup, and store the invoice and licence text next to your files. The licence is what proves your right to commercial use tomorrow in front of your client or partner — and it is the first thing you will be asked to show if a question ever comes up.

  1. Pay, download and communicate inside the platform only
  2. Read the licence in full before paying, not after
  3. Judge the seller by verified reviews and a real preview
  4. Beware the "original" at an impossible price: usually pirated
  5. Keep the invoice, the licence text and a backup copy
Remote marketing

Remote marketing: how does a brand in Amman, Kuwait or Doha work with an Arab agency it never meets?

A brand in Amman, Kuwait or Doha does not need an agency in its own city to run a strong content campaign: the brief is written online, creators are chosen by your audience's dialect, and filming, review and reporting run remotely to written standards. What separates a remote engagement that works from one that fails is not the distance — it is the writing: a clear brief, a deliverables list, usage rights, and a report after publishing.

Start from the market, not the agency: your audience in Jordan, Kuwait and Qatar speaks a Gulf/Levantine dialect and discovers brands from short videos — so the creator has to speak your audience's dialect and live its context, not just be famous. Then ask the agency three things before any agreement: shortlists with names and work samples before filming, a written quote detailing scope and deliverables, and a results report after publishing with tracked links and codes. The agency that answers all three in writing is the one that can run your campaign remotely.

And protection in remote work is the same as in any digital market: everything inside one channel — brief, approvals, payment and files — a written agreement before filming that sets usage, duration and territory, and payment tied to an approved delivery, not a verbal promise. What is the difference between a digital product and a digital service?

A digital product exists before you buy it: an ebook, a template or a recorded course that you buy as-is and receive instantly. A digital service is performed after you order it, specifically for you: identity design, video editing, content writing. The first is delivered in seconds; the second has a delivery date agreed with its provider — and the market brings both together in one place.

How do I receive what I bought?

The moment payment completes inside the platform, the download link appears in your account and reaches your email too, together with the invoice and the product's usage licence text. Keep a backup of your files: re-downloading from your account is available, but your own organised archive saves you the searching later.

What does a usage licence mean, and why should I care?

The licence is the written permission that defines what you may do with the product: personal or commercial use, for one project or many, and whether it covers your clients or not. Reselling the product itself or distributing it is always prohibited unless the licence explicitly states otherwise. Read the licence before paying, not after — every product in the market displays its licence in clear text.
