02 — Digital market guides

Tips and know-how for any digital buyer or seller

The market is still being prepared, but the knowledge does not have to wait: three short, practical guides from our daily work in digital content and services.

Guide one

What is a digital product — and how do you start selling one?

A digital product is anything sold and delivered electronically — no shipping, no stock, no repeated manufacturing: an e-book, a recorded course, a design or presentation template, print-ready files, licensed photos or music, or ready-made sheets and tools. Its core advantage: it is made once and can be sold many times.

Start from a skill you already have

The strongest digital products grow out of something you already do well: design, writing, organising, or years of experience in a field. Ask yourself: what is the problem people always ask me about? That is usually your first product.

Make it specific, not general

"Social media templates" is a title that gets lost; "30 Instagram post templates for restaurants" is a clear product: the buyer understands exactly what they get, and you know exactly who to market it to. Specificity sells — and cuts questions and refunds.

Prepare it as if it will sell a thousand times

  • Organised files with clear names, in the formats buyers will actually need.
  • An honest preview: images or a sample showing exactly what will be delivered — no exaggeration.
  • A complete description: what is inside, what is not, and which software or devices it works with.
  • A written licence: personal or commercial use? One project or unlimited projects?

Choose the right sales channel

You can sell from your own store if you have an audience and a payment method, or through an electronic marketplace that gathers sellers and buyers — which spares you building a store and payment gateways, and puts you in front of a ready audience from day one. Many sellers start on a marketplace, then add their own channels once their name grows.

Delivery and support are your reputation

With digital products, the baseline expectation is instant delivery after payment. Your courteous replies to buyers' questions, and updating the product when something changes, are what build your rating — and a rating is a seller's real capital in any market.

Guide two

How do you price a digital product?

There is no magic number; the right price is calculated from the value the buyer receives — not from the hours you spent making it, because the product will sell many times without you redoing the work. These are the factors that actually set the price:

Value before cost

If your template saves the buyer two days of work, or your product helps them win a client, its price is measured against that. Buyers pay for the outcome and the time saved, not for your effort.

Study your category's range

Look at similar products in the same category at the same quality level, and place yourself inside that range: higher if your product is broader, better made or better supported; lower if you are still building your name and reviews. A price far outside the range — in either direction — makes buyers suspicious.

Cover your real costs

  • Sales-channel and payment-gateway fees — a percentage taken from every transaction.
  • Asset licences: fonts, elements or photos you used under a commercial licence.
  • Support time: answering buyers and fixing issues is part of the product's cost.

Build tiers instead of one price

A basic version at an entry price, and a fuller "Pro" version with more files, a wider licence or longer support. Buyers choose what fits their need, you earn more from those who need more — without losing those on a smaller budget.

Do not join the race to the bottom

A very low price erodes trust before it lifts sales. Better: a fair price from the start, limited launch offers if you want an initial push, then watch sales and questions and adjust. Pricing is a decision you revisit and tune, not a final verdict.

Guide three

How do you buy safely in digital marketplaces?

Digital buying is safe when the deal happens inside the platform and you read the product's terms before paying. Most problems come from two situations: a deal that moves outside the market, or a usage licence nobody read. These are the rules that protect you:

Always transact inside the platform

Paying, messaging and delivery inside the market creates a record of the transaction and a reference point if a problem occurs. Any seller who asks for a direct transfer or off-platform contact "to save the fees" is a red flag — walk away; those fees are the price of your protection.

Read the usage licence before you pay

  • Personal or commercial? The difference decides whether you can use the product in client work or only for yourself.
  • Number of projects and copies: many licences limit use to a single project or a set number of seats.
  • Resale and redistribution: buying a template does not give you the right to resell it or give it away.

If the licence is not clear on the product page, ask the seller before paying — their written answer inside the platform is part of your agreement.

Judge the seller before the product

A seller with a precise description, clear products and professional replies to questions is far safer than an empty page with a tempting price. Reviews and comments from earlier buyers are the shortest route to a judgement.

Beware the "too-cheap original"

Well-known digital products at unbelievable prices are usually pirated copies: they may carry harmful files, and using them in commercial work definitely exposes you to claims from the rights holder. If the price makes no sense, the product is not genuine.

Keep your proof and your files

After buying: keep the invoice and the licence text as it stood at purchase time, download your files and keep a backup of your own. Once delivered, safekeeping a digital product is your responsibility — losing files from your device cannot always be fixed by re-downloading.
